译者序言
奉普慈特慈的真主之名，一切赞颂，全归真主——众世界的养主；
愿真主赐福与平安予我们的领袖穆罕默德（愿主福安之），以及他的家
属与圣门弟子们，直到复生日。
生活在这个世界上，我们常常被太多的事情裹挟着向前。白天奔
波，夜晚思索，我们以为只要日程足够满、目标足够明晰、脚步够快，
就能填补内心的空缺。
但有时候，我们却依然在最丰盈的表面下，感到一种说不出口的
干涸。那是一种无声的疲惫：不是没有动力，而是心，不知道该安放
在哪里。
就在那样一个阶段，我读到了这样一句话：
“Detach from the dunya so your heart can attach to what is eternal.”
“让你的心从尘世中抽离，才能归属永恒。”
——Yasmin Mogahed，《Reclaim Your Heart》
这一句话仿佛一下点中了我内心最深的缝隙。那一刻，我意识到：
原来问题从来不只是“过得怎么样”，而是“我把我的心，交给了谁”。
我是在加拿大读到这本书的。那时的我，一边学习语言，一边适
应新的生活节奏，也一边努力继续自己在 Islam 传播与心理讲解方面的
工作。
我是一位穆斯林，也是一名内容创作者，在中国经营着自己的公
众号，长期翻译并讲解与信仰相关的图文、视频与书籍。我喜欢讲书，
也喜欢讲人心。我一直在寻找一本作品，它能够跨越文化、语言与伤
口，真正地温柔触碰到我们深层的精神问题。
《Reclaim Your Heart》就是那本书。

I

当我翻开它，一字一句读下去，我几乎立刻就意识到：这是一本
我不能只为自己读完的书。
它像是一封迟来的信，从远方悄然寄来，却恰好落在我最需要回
应的那一刻。Yasmin 的文字没有铺陈华丽的词藻，也不刻意制造感动，
却在每一页都问着我：你的心，到底属于谁？
她不是在“教”我们，而是在陪我们走过——用她自己经历过的
破碎与归向 Allah，帮助我们看见那个在生活中反复跌倒、但仍然想靠
近真主的自己。
那份温柔而坚定的共鸣，让我无法仅仅把这本书读完。我决定把
它翻译出来，献给更多同样在挣扎中的人。
因为我知道：这本书不是只为某个人而写的，它应该被更多的心
读到。
它不是教我们如何活得更像别人期待的样子，而是提醒我们活出
心灵最深处真主最喜爱的模样——在痛苦中认出真主，在失望中认出
依附错位的自己，在人生的种种挫败中，回到那个唯一不会让我们失
望的归属。
我们必须重拾自己的心。因为一颗遗失了归属的心，是无法真正
安顿的。《古兰经》中这样提醒我们：

In the Name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.
All praise is due to Allah, the Lord of the worlds. May peace and blessings
be upon our Prophet Muhammad, and upon his family and his noble
companions, until the Day of Judgment.
Living in this world, we are often swept forward by an endless tide of
responsibilities and goals.
By day we rush, by night we reflect. We come to believe that as long
as our schedules stay full, our goals are clear, and our steps are fast
enough—we will somehow fill the void inside.
But sometimes, even at the peak of outward abundance, there remains
a quiet thirst within. A soundless exhaustion. Not due to lack of ambition,
but because the heart no longer knows where to rest.
It was in such a moment that I came across a sentence:
“Detach from the dunya so your heart can attach to what is eternal.”
——Yasmin Mogahed, Reclaim Your Heart
It felt like the words landed directly in a deep and hidden crack in my
soul. I realized then that the question was never merely about how I was
doing—but rather, to whom I had entrusted my heart.
I first read this book while living in Canada. At the time, I was
learning the language, adapting to a new rhythm of life, and continuing my
work in Islamic education and spiritual-psychological content.
As a Muslim, and as someone actively sharing reflections through
public writing and multimedia translation in China, I had long been
V

looking for a work that could bridge cultures, transcend language, and
speak with tenderness to the wounded places of the soul.
Reclaim Your Heart was that book.
From the first few pages, I knew: this was not a book I could read for
myself alone.
It felt like a letter long delayed, finally arriving just when my heart
needed an answer. Yasmin’s words are not adorned with elaborate language,
nor do they try to force inspiration. Instead, each page quietly asks:
To whom does your heart belong?
She does not teach us from above, but walks with us—using her own
path of brokenness and return to Allah to help us recognize the part of
ourselves that keeps falling, yet still longs to draw closer to our Lord.
That gentle yet firm resonance made it impossible for me to keep this
book to myself.
I felt compelled to translate it, to offer it to those walking through the
same struggles.
Because I know this book was never meant for one person alone. It is
a message that deserves to be read by many hearts.
It does not teach us how to become the version of ourselves others
expect.It reminds us to become the version that Allah loves most—the one
that recognizes Him through pain, sees our misplaced dependencies in
disappointment, and returns through every loss to the One who never fails
us.
We must reclaim our hearts.
Because a heart that has lost its rightful belonging can never truly be

at peace. The Qur’an reminds us:
“ The Day when neither wealth nor children will be of any
benefit—except for the one who comes to Allah with a sound heart.”
(Surah Ash-Shu’ara, 26:88–89)
And what is a sound heart?
The scholars explain that qalbun saleem is not merely a heart free

It is a heart free from spiritual disease—untainted by arrogance, envy,
or resentment.
It is a heart that may have been wounded in this world, but has not
been corrupted by it. A heart that remains anchored in its longing for its
Lord.
It is the heart that, despite having tasted loss, chooses not to turn bitter.
That, after all its misplaced attachments, still chooses to return to Allah.
That, after shedding the illusions of the world, still holds on to softness,
and to sincerity.
This is why I translated this book. Not just to carry over its
words—but to carry its invitation. An invitation to reconnect. To remember.
To come home.
I have tried, in this translation, to preserve not only the message but
the rhythm, tone, and warmth of the original. I have strived to make it
accessible to Chinese readers in a way that honors both the beauty of the
author’s words and the sacredness of the truths they carry.
This is not a book meant to be academically dissected. It is a book
meant to be held, and felt, and slowly returned to.

离别是痛苦的，失去更是如此。
几周前，我曾问过一个问题：
“为什么人们必须离开彼此？”这个
问题让我深入思考了自己一生中最深刻的体悟和挣扎。

这个世界有一个奇妙的特性。那些让我们痛苦的特质，同样也是
给予我们慰藉的特质——这里没有什么是永恒的。
这意味着什么？

我们常说，这个世界不完美。这的确如此。它既不完全美好，也
不完全糟糕。
Allah（愿荣耀归于祂）在《古兰经》中告诉我们一个极其深刻的
真理：
“艰难与容易是相伴的。”
——《古兰经·开拓章》94:5
小时候，我对这节经文的理解是错误的。我曾以为，它的意思是：
困难之后会迎来容易。
、

5

重拾你的心
换句话说，我以为人生是由好时光和坏时光组成的，坏时光过后，
好时光便会到来。我以为，生活要么是全然美好，要么是全然黑暗。
但这并不是这节经文真正的含义。
Allah 所说的是，“困难之后”
，并非“困难之后才会有容易”，而
是“困难之中就有容易”。

如果我们仔细回顾自己人生中的困难时刻，我们会发现其中也充
满了许多美好。

我认为，我们往往陷入一个误区，那就是错误地相信这个世界可
以是完美的——要么完全美好，要么完全糟糕。然而，这并不是今世
（Dunya）的本质。真正的完美，属于后世（Akhirah）。
天 堂（ Jannah ） 是 绝 对 完 美的 ， 在那 里 没有 一 丝 痛苦 。 地 狱
（Jahannam，愿 Allah 保护我们）是完全黑暗的，在那里没有一丝善良。

一切都会过去。

will be okay.” Because maybe it won’t—unless you return your heart to
Allah.
Not to say “You’re already good enough.”Maybe you aren’t. Maybe
life hasn’t broken you open yet.
Not to say “It’s okay to be lost.”Because not everyone is willing to
wake up.
What I want to say is:You are allowed to break—so long as you break
toward Him.

You can collapse. You can empty every ambition, every habit, every
plan. You can weep.But what you cannot do… is continue walking

The Prophet ‫ﷺ‬said:
“Truly, in the body there is a piece of flesh. If it is sound, the whole

(Sahih al-Bukhari)
If you lose your heart—you haven’t just lost peace.You’ve lost your
self.
I don’t know what season of life you’re in.Maybe you’re stuck in a
marriage.Maybe you’re drowning in grief.Maybe you’ve stopped believing
wholeness is even possible.
But let me ask you one question, the question Yasmin began
with:Who have you given your heart to?
If you don’t know the answer, it’s okay.This book was written to help
you remember.
Because returning to Allah is not a change in behavior.It is a return to
your origin.
The Quran reminds us of the covenant we made with our Lord before
we were ever born:
“And [remember] when your Lord took from the children of Adam—from
